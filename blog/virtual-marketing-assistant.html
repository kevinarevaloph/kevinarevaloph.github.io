What Does a Virtual Marketing Assistant Do? A Guide for Businesses

What marketing VAs do, what tasks you can delegate, and how they can support an existing marketing team

A virtual marketing assistant helps businesses handle marketing tasks remotely. That can include SEO and content, social media, email marketing, lead generation, website updates, research, and reporting.


The exact role depends on the business and the person's skills. Some marketing VAs focus on one area, while others support several parts of a marketing operation.

For a business owner, director, or marketing manager, the bigger question is often: What marketing work can I actually delegate to a virtual marketing assistant?

This guide explains what a marketing VA does, which tasks you can delegate, how the role fits into an existing team, and when outside marketing support may make sense.

What Is a Virtual Marketing Assistant?

A virtual marketing assistant is a remote professional who helps businesses with marketing-related tasks. The work can range from content and SEO to social media, email, lead generation, website management, research, and reporting.

Depending on the business and the person's skills, a marketing VA may help with tasks such as:

  • SEO and content
  • Social media
  • Email marketing
  • Lead generation
  • Website management
  • Marketing research
  • Reporting and data organization

A virtual marketing assistant is not automatically a marketing strategist. There is an important difference between executing defined marketing work and owning the planning and strategic decisions behind that work.

A business may have a marketing director or strategist who sets priorities and makes larger decisions, while the VA handles specific execution tasks.

Before hiring a marketing VA, it is useful to decide what the person will be responsible for, what requires approval, and which decisions remain with the internal team.

Marketing virtual assistants can also differ in how specialized their work is. Some provide broad support across several areas, while others focus on specific skills such as SEO, paid advertising, content, or design. HubSpot provides a useful overview of the difference between generalist and specialist virtual assistants.

What Does a Virtual Marketing Assistant Do?

A marketing VA may help with the execution side of SEO, such as research, content preparation, formatting, and updates. For a broader overview of how SEO helps search engines understand content and how businesses can improve their presence in search, see Google's SEO Starter Guide.

They can support a wide range of recurring marketing activities, including SEO, content, social media, email marketing, lead generation, website updates, and research. The exact responsibilities depend on the business, but current marketing VA roles commonly combine several of these areas.

SEO and Content

SEO and content work can include both research and execution. A marketing VA may help prepare content, organize information, and complete defined SEO tasks.

Common tasks include:

  • Keyword research
  • Topic research
  • Content research
  • Blog writing
  • Content briefs
  • On-page SEO
  • Internal linking
  • Content updates
  • Content formatting
  • Blog publishing
  • Content calendars

For example, a marketing VA may research keywords for an upcoming article, organize the findings into a content brief, format the finished article, add internal links, and publish the approved post.

The marketing strategy and final priorities may still belong to the business owner, marketing manager, or SEO specialist.

Social Media

Social media support often involves preparing, organizing, and publishing content according to an approved plan.

Common tasks include:

  • Preparing social media content
  • Formatting posts
  • Creating content calendars
  • Scheduling approved posts
  • Publishing content
  • Organizing social media assets
  • Basic engagement support
  • Tracking social media performance

A VA can help keep social media work moving by making sure approved content is organized and scheduled consistently.

Depending on the business, the internal team may still handle brand direction, messaging, campaign strategy, and sensitive customer conversations.

Email Marketing

Email marketing involves many recurring tasks that can be organized and delegated when the process is clearly defined.

Common tasks include:

  • Preparing newsletters
  • Formatting email campaigns
  • Scheduling emails
  • Organizing email lists
  • Updating campaign information
  • Tracking basic email performance
  • Supporting email campaign workflows

A marketing VA may prepare an email inside a marketing platform, check the formatting, organize the audience, and schedule the campaign after it has been approved.

Strategic decisions such as campaign goals, audience strategy, offers, positioning, and overall email strategy may remain with the marketing team.

Lead Generation

Lead generation support can involve researching potential customers and keeping prospect information organized.

Common tasks include:

  • Prospect research
  • Building lead lists
  • LinkedIn research
  • Organizing prospect information
  • Updating lead tracking systems
  • Supporting outreach activities
  • Gathering information about potential customers

A marketing VA can help with the research and organization required to support lead generation.

However, supporting lead generation does not necessarily mean owning the sales strategy. Decisions about ideal customers, sales messaging, qualification, offers, and outreach strategy may still belong to the sales or marketing team.

Website Management

Website management can include recurring content updates and publishing tasks that do not require full web development skills.

Common tasks include:

  • Publishing blog posts
  • Formatting website content
  • Uploading images
  • Updating existing pages
  • Adding or editing basic website content
  • Managing website content in a CMS
  • Supporting basic WordPress tasks

For example, a VA may upload a new blog article, add images, format headings, update an existing page, or make other approved content changes.

This is different from web development. More technical work, such as building complex functionality or developing custom systems, may require a developer.

Research and Reporting

Research and reporting help businesses organize information needed to make marketing decisions.

Common tasks include:

  • Competitor research
  • Market research
  • Prospect research
  • Content research
  • Data gathering
  • Campaign reports
  • Performance tracking
  • Marketing data organization

A marketing VA can gather information, organize performance data, prepare reports, and make it easier for a manager or business owner to review what is happening across their marketing activities.

What Marketing Tasks Can You Delegate?

One useful way to think about delegation is through the Pareto Principle, or 80/20 rule. The principle suggests that a relatively small portion of inputs can produce a large share of the results, which can help businesses think about where their time and resources are having the greatest impact. In a marketing context, this can mean identifying the work that requires your direct attention and delegating repeatable tasks that others can handle. The 80/20 ratio should not be treated as a fixed rule, but it can be a useful framework for reviewing priorities and deciding where support may be valuable.

A useful starting point is to look for marketing tasks that are:

  • Repetitive
  • Clearly defined
  • Process-driven
  • Time-consuming
  • Easy to review
  • Important but not necessarily strategic

Here are some examples:

Area Example Task
Content Format and publish approved blog posts
SEO Research keywords for planned content
Social media Schedule approved posts
Email Prepare and schedule newsletters
Lead generation Research and organize prospects
Website Update existing website content
Reporting Organize marketing performance data

These are examples, not rules. What you can delegate depends on your team, processes, goals, and the skills of the person handling the work.

One useful question to ask is:

Is this task taking time away from something only I or my team needs to handle?

If the answer is yes, the task may be worth reviewing to see whether some or all of it can be delegated.

How Does a Marketing VA Fit Into a Business?

A virtual marketing assistant does not have to replace an existing employee or marketing department. In many businesses, the VA simply supports the existing team.

A simple workflow can look like this:

  1. Business leadership
  2. Marketing strategy
  3. Planning
  4. VA execution
  5. Reporting and review
  6. Optimization

For example, a marketing director may want to publish two blog posts each month. The marketing VA can help with keyword research, content research, formatting, publishing, and the content calendar.

The marketing director can continue to handle larger marketing priorities while the VA supports the execution work.

Before starting, it helps to define:

  • What the VA owns
  • What the internal team owns
  • What requires approval
  • Which tools the VA can access
  • How work will be tracked
  • How often progress will be reviewed

Marketing VA vs. Freelancer vs. Agency

A virtual marketing assistant is one way to get outside marketing support. Freelancers and marketing agencies can also provide support, but the type of work and working relationship may be different.

Option Typical Role May Fit When
Virtual marketing assistant Ongoing support with defined marketing tasks There is recurring workload and the required skills are defined
Freelancer Specialized or project-based work A specific skill or project is required
Marketing agency Broader marketing services and team support The scope and complexity of the marketing needs require broader support

These categories can overlap. The better approach is to focus on the actual work that needs to be completed.

Consider questions such as:

  1. What tasks need to be completed?
  2. How often does the work need to be done?
  3. What skills are required?
  4. How many strategic decisions are involved?
  5. How much involvement will the internal team need?
  6. Do you need one person or a larger team?

When Should You Hire a Virtual Marketing Assistant?

There is no single point when every business should hire a marketing VA. The right time depends on the amount and type of marketing work your business needs.

A business may want to consider outside support when:

  • Marketing tasks regularly fall behind
  • Business leaders spend too much time on routine marketing work
  • A marketing employee needs help with execution
  • Content publishing is inconsistent
  • Social media scheduling is delayed
  • Lead research takes too much time
  • Website updates keep getting postponed
  • The team has defined tasks but not enough time

The key question is whether marketing work is being delayed because the business does not have enough time or resources to complete it.

If that is happening regularly, outside support may be worth evaluating.

What Should You Look for When Hiring a Marketing VA?

The skills you need will depend on the work you want the person to handle. Some useful areas to consider include:

  • Marketing knowledge
  • Strong writing skills
  • SEO knowledge
  • Research skills
  • Organization
  • Attention to detail
  • Familiarity with relevant marketing platforms
  • Clear communication
  • Reliability
  • Ability to follow processes
  • Ability to document completed work
  • Understanding of the business and its audience

Technical skills are only one part of the role. Clear communication, reliable execution, and the ability to follow defined processes can also be important.

It is also important to be clear about the scope of the role. The person should know what they are expected to do, what they can decide independently, and what needs approval.

How to Get Started With a Virtual Marketing Assistant

You do not need to outsource all of your marketing at once. A practical approach is to start with tasks that are easy to define and useful to delegate.

1. List the Marketing Tasks Taking Too Much Time

Start by writing down the marketing tasks that regularly take time away from higher-priority work.

Look for recurring tasks such as content formatting, research, scheduling, publishing, reporting, and website updates.

2. Separate Recurring Work From Strategic Decisions

Separate the work that follows a repeatable process from work that requires strategic decisions.

This makes it easier to determine what can be delegated and what should remain with the business owner or marketing team.

3. Document the Process

Write down how the task should be completed. This could include instructions, examples, checklists, links, naming conventions, and approval steps.

Clear documentation makes recurring work easier to delegate and review.

4. Define Responsibilities and Access

Decide what the VA can access and what they are responsible for. Only provide the access needed to complete the assigned work.

Also define which tasks require approval before anything is published or sent.

5. Set Communication and Reporting Expectations

Decide how work will be assigned, where tasks will be tracked, and how progress will be reported.

A simple process can prevent confusion and make it easier for everyone to see what has been completed and what still needs attention.

6. Review the Work and Improve the Process

After the first few tasks are completed, review what worked and what did not.

If the process can be made clearer or more efficient, update the documentation and continue improving it.

This approach is consistent with the broader principle of delegating repeatable, lower-impact work so business owners and marketing teams can focus more time on higher-value activities.
