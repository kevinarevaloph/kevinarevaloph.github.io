What Does a Virtual Marketing Assistant Do? A Guide for Businesses

What marketing VAs do, what tasks you can delegate, and how they can support an existing marketing team

A virtual marketing assistant helps businesses manage marketing tasks remotely.

Depending on their skills, they may support SEO and content, social media, email marketing, lead generation, website updates, research, reporting, and other day-to-day marketing work.


The exact role depends on the business and the person's experience. Some marketing VAs focus on one area, while others provide broader support across several parts of a marketing operation.


For a business owner, director, or marketing manager, the bigger question is often:

"What marketing work can I actually delegate to a virtual marketing assistant?"

This guide explains what a virtual marketing assistant does, which marketing tasks you can delegate, how a marketing VA can work with an existing team, and when outsourced marketing support may make sense.

What Is a Virtual Marketing Assistant?

A virtual marketing assistant is a remote professional who helps a business with marketing work.

The work is usually completed online using tools such as content management systems, social media platforms, email marketing software, project management tools, and shared documents.

Depending on the role, a marketing virtual assistant may help with:

  • SEO and content
  • Social media
  • Email marketing
  • Lead generation
  • Website management
  • Marketing research
  • Reporting and data organization

Marketing virtual assistants can also differ in how specialized their work is. Some provide broad support across several areas, while others focus on specific skills such as SEO, paid advertising, content, or design. HubSpot provides a useful overview of the difference between generalist and specialist virtual assistants.

A marketing VA is not automatically a marketing strategist.

Some businesses need someone to execute an existing marketing plan. Others may need a digital marketing virtual assistant with broader skills who can also help with planning and recommendations.

That difference matters when defining the role.

Before hiring someone, decide what work you need help with and how much responsibility the person should have.

What Does a Virtual Marketing Assistant Do?

A virtual marketing assistant can support many parts of your marketing operation. The right responsibilities depend on your goals, existing team, and the person's skills.

A marketing VA may help with the execution side of SEO, such as research, content preparation, formatting, and updates. For a broader overview of how SEO helps search engines understand content and how businesses can improve their presence in search, see Google's SEO Starter Guide.

SEO and Content

Content marketing involves many tasks before and after an article is published.

A marketing VA with the right skills may help with:

  • Keyword research
  • Topic research
  • Content research
  • Blog writing
  • Content briefs
  • On-page SEO
  • Internal linking
  • Content updates
  • Content formatting
  • Blog publishing
  • Content calendars

For example, your marketing team may decide which topics support the business strategy. A digital marketing VA can then research keywords, prepare content, format the article, and publish it.

The level of SEO support depends on the person's experience.

A VA may be able to handle keyword research and on-page tasks without being responsible for your entire SEO strategy.

Social Media

Social media management involves more than creating posts.

A marketing assistant may help with:

  • Preparing social media content
  • Formatting posts
  • Creating content calendars
  • Scheduling approved posts
  • Publishing content
  • Organizing social media assets
  • Basic engagement support
  • Tracking social media performance

This can be useful when a business already has a social media plan but does not have enough time to execute it consistently.

The business or marketing team can continue to handle larger decisions while the VA manages defined day-to-day tasks.

Email Marketing

Email marketing also involves recurring work that can be delegated.

Depending on their skills and responsibilities, a virtual assistant for marketing may help with:

  • Preparing newsletters
  • Formatting email campaigns
  • Scheduling emails
  • Organizing email lists
  • Updating campaign information
  • Tracking basic email performance
  • Supporting email campaign workflows

For example, a marketing manager may decide what the campaign should communicate and who should receive it. The VA can help prepare and schedule the campaign.

The exact responsibilities should be agreed on before the work begins.

Lead Generation

Lead generation often includes research and organization work that can take significant time.

A marketing VA may support tasks such as:

  • Prospect research
  • Building lead lists
  • LinkedIn research
  • Organizing prospect information
  • Updating lead tracking systems
  • Supporting outreach activities
  • Gathering information about potential customers

There is an important difference between supporting lead generation and owning the entire sales strategy.

A VA can research and organize prospects without being responsible for the company's complete sales process.

Website Management

Website updates are often important but easy to postpone when a team is busy.

A remote marketing assistant may help with:

  • Publishing blog posts
  • Formatting website content
  • Uploading images
  • Updating existing pages
  • Adding or editing basic website content
  • Managing website content in a CMS
  • Supporting basic WordPress tasks

This type of support is different from web development.

If you need custom development, advanced technical work, or major website changes, you may need a developer or another specialist.

Research and Reporting

Marketing decisions often require information from several sources.

A marketing VA may help gather and organize information through:

  • Competitor research
  • Market research
  • Prospect research
  • Content research
  • Data gathering
  • Campaign reports
  • Performance tracking
  • Marketing data organization

This type of marketing support can reduce the amount of time your team spends collecting and organizing information.

What Marketing Tasks Can You Delegate?

Not every marketing task should be outsourced.

A useful starting point is to look for work that is:

  • Repetitive
  • Clearly defined
  • Process-driven
  • Time-consuming
  • Easy to review
  • Important but not necessarily strategic

For example:

Marketing Area Possible Task to Delegate
Content Format and publish approved blog posts
SEO Research keywords for planned content
Social media Schedule approved posts
Email Prepare and schedule newsletters
Lead generation Research and organize prospects
Website Update existing website content
Reporting Organize marketing performance data

These are examples, not rules.

The right tasks to delegate depend on your team, processes, goals, and the person's skills.

One useful question is:

"Is this task taking time away from something only I or my team needs to handle?"

If the answer is yes, the task may be worth reviewing for delegation.

This is where small business marketing support can be especially useful. A business may not need a full marketing department to handle every recurring task. It may simply need reliable help with clearly defined work.

This approach is consistent with the broader principle of delegating repeatable, lower-impact work so business owners and marketing teams can focus more time on higher-value activities. HubSpot also recommends defining clear outcomes and documenting recurring processes when working with a virtual assistant. See its guide to delegating work to virtual assistants.

How Does a Marketing VA Fit Into a Business?

A marketing VA does not have to replace an existing marketing employee or department.

In many cases, the role is there to support the people already responsible for marketing.

A simple workflow might look like this:

  1. Business leadership
  2. Marketing strategy
  3. Planning
  4. VA execution
  5. Reporting and review
  6. Optimization

For example, a marketing director may decide that the company needs to publish two blog posts each month.

The marketing VA could then help with keyword research, content research, formatting, publishing, and the content calendar.

The marketing director can remain responsible for larger marketing priorities and decisions.

This type of setup works best when responsibilities are clear.

Before work begins, define:

  • What the VA owns
  • What the internal team owns
  • What requires approval
  • Which tools the VA can access
  • How work will be tracked
  • How often progress will be reviewed

Clear responsibilities make remote collaboration easier and reduce confusion about who is responsible for each task.

Marketing VA vs. Freelancer vs. Agency

A virtual marketing assistant, freelancer, and marketing agency can all provide outsourced marketing support, but they usually serve different needs.

Option Typical Role What to Consider
Virtual marketing assistant Ongoing support with defined marketing tasks Recurring workload and required skills
Freelancer Specialized or project-based work Specific skill or project requirements
Marketing agency Broader marketing services and team support Scope and complexity of your marketing needs

These categories can overlap.

For example, a freelancer may provide ongoing support. A marketing VA may have specialized skills. An agency may handle both strategy and execution.

Instead of focusing only on the job title, start with the work you need completed.

Ask yourself:

  1. What tasks need to be done?
  2. How often do they need to be done?
  3. What skills are required?
  4. Does the person need to make strategic decisions?
  5. How much involvement will my existing team have?
  6. Do I need one person or a larger team?

The answers can help you determine what type of outside support fits your situation.

When Should You Hire a Virtual Marketing Assistant?

There is no single point when every business should hire a virtual marketing assistant.

However, certain situations may indicate that additional support is worth considering.

You may want to explore this option if:

  • Marketing tasks regularly fall behind.
  • Business leaders spend too much time on routine marketing work.
  • Your marketing employee needs help with execution.
  • Content publishing is inconsistent.
  • Social media scheduling keeps getting delayed.
  • Lead research takes too much time.
  • Website updates keep getting postponed.
  • Your team has clearly defined marketing tasks but not enough time to handle them.

The key question is not simply whether your team is busy.

Ask whether important marketing work is being delayed because your current team does not have enough time or resources to complete it.

If that happens regularly, outside support may be worth evaluating.

What Should You Look for When Hiring a Marketing VA?

The right skills depend on the work you want to delegate.

Someone handling SEO content will need different skills from someone focused mainly on email marketing or social media.

Depending on your needs, look for:

  • Marketing knowledge
  • Strong writing skills
  • SEO knowledge
  • Research skills
  • Organization
  • Attention to detail
  • Familiarity with relevant marketing platforms
  • Clear communication
  • Reliability
  • Ability to follow processes
  • Ability to document completed work
  • Understanding of your business and audience

Technical skills are only part of the picture.

A good working relationship also requires clear communication and expectations. The person should understand what needs to be done, what the expected result looks like, and when to ask questions.

Most importantly, make sure the scope of work is clear.

If you expect one person to handle strategy, content, social media, email, SEO, and lead generation, define those responsibilities clearly from the start.

How to Get Started With a Virtual Marketing Assistant

You do not need to outsource your entire marketing operation.

Start with the work that is easiest to define and most useful to delegate.

1. List the Marketing Tasks Taking Too Much Time

Write down the recurring marketing tasks your team handles each week or month.

Include tasks that are often delayed or pushed to the bottom of the list.

2. Separate Recurring Work From Strategic Decisions

Some marketing tasks require your judgment.

Others simply need to be completed correctly and on time.

Start by identifying work that does not require you or your marketing leader to make the final strategic decision.

3. Document the Process

Explain how the task should be completed.

Include examples, deadlines, tools, approval steps, and expected results where needed.

You do not need a complicated manual. A simple checklist can be enough to get started.

4. Define Responsibilities and Access

Decide what the VA can handle independently and what requires approval.

Also decide which tools and accounts the person needs to access.

5. Set Communication and Reporting Expectations

Agree on how you will communicate and how completed work will be tracked.

You might use a shared task list, project management system, or regular progress report.

For businesses using multiple tools, a VA may also provide marketing automation support or help keep basic marketing operations organized.

6. Review the Work and Improve the Process

Delegation is not a one-time setup.

Review the work regularly. If a process is unclear, improve it. If a task is working well, keep the process simple.

As your workflow develops, you can decide whether additional marketing tasks should be delegated.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Virtual Marketing Assistant?

A virtual marketing assistant is a remote professional who helps with marketing tasks. Depending on their skills, they may support SEO, content, social media, email marketing, lead generation, website management, research, reporting, and other marketing activities.

What Does a Marketing VA Do?

A marketing VA handles defined marketing tasks that a business needs completed. These may include content research, social media scheduling, email campaign support, lead research, website updates, and marketing reporting.

The exact role depends on the business and the VA's skills.

Can a VA Help With SEO?

Yes. A marketing VA with SEO skills may help with keyword research, content research, on-page SEO, internal linking, content updates, and publishing.

However, not every VA is an SEO specialist. Make sure the person's skills match the level of SEO work you need.

Can a Marketing VA Manage Social Media?

A marketing VA may help prepare, schedule, and publish social media content. They may also help maintain a content calendar and track basic performance.

Whether they should develop the social media strategy depends on their experience and the responsibilities you assign.

Can a Marketing VA Help With Lead Generation?

Yes. A marketing VA may help with prospect research, lead list building, LinkedIn research, lead tracking, and outreach support.

This is different from owning the company's full sales strategy.

What Is the Difference Between a Marketing VA and a General VA?

A general VA may handle administrative and business support tasks across different areas.

A marketing VA focuses specifically on marketing work. Their skills may include content, SEO, social media, email marketing, lead generation, research, or website management.

How Does a Marketing VA Work With an Existing Marketing Team?

A marketing VA can take on defined execution tasks while the internal team focuses on strategy, planning, approvals, and larger decisions.

The exact setup depends on the team's structure and the work being delegated.

When Should a Business Hire a Virtual Marketing Assistant?

Consider a virtual marketing assistant when important marketing tasks regularly fall behind, your team spends too much time on routine work, or you have recurring marketing tasks that need an owner.

Start by identifying the specific work you need help with rather than trying to outsource everything at once.

Conclusion: Is a Virtual Marketing Assistant Right for Your Business?

A virtual marketing assistant can be a practical way to manage defined marketing tasks when your business or marketing team does not have enough time to handle everything internally.

The goal is not to hand over everything.

Start with clearly defined work that can be completed remotely. Then review the process and decide whether additional marketing support makes sense.

The right setup depends on your goals, team structure, workload, and the skills you need. Whether you need help with content, SEO, social media, email, lead generation, website updates, or research, clearly defined responsibilities can make marketing execution easier to manage.
