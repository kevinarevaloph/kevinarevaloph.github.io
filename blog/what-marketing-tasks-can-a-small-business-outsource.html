What Marketing Tasks Can a Small Business Outsource?

A practical guide to outsourcing recurring marketing tasks and deciding what to keep in-house

Marketing can quickly become a long list of tasks.

A business owner may need to manage blog posts, social media, emails, SEO, website updates, lead research, and reports while still trying to run the business.

You don't have to handle every marketing task yourself.

Many small businesses can outsource recurring marketing work to a virtual marketing assistant, freelancer, specialist, or agency. Common examples include content writing, social media management, website updates, research, lead generation support, and reporting.

If you're considering ongoing marketing support, a virtual marketing assistant can help take recurring tasks off your plate while giving you more time to focus on running your business.

The key is knowing which tasks to delegate and which responsibilities should stay with your business.

In this guide, we'll look at the marketing tasks you can outsource, what you should keep in-house, and how to decide where to start.

What Marketing Tasks Can a Small Business Outsource?

Small businesses can outsource many recurring marketing tasks, including:

  • Content writing and management
  • SEO support
  • Social media management
  • Email marketing
  • Website updates
  • Lead generation support
  • Marketing research
  • Competitor research
  • Marketing reporting
  • Marketing operations and administrative tasks

The best tasks to outsource are usually repeatable, clearly defined, time-consuming, and easy to review.

A useful rule is:

Outsource execution when possible. Keep important business decisions under your control.

For example, writing and formatting a weekly blog post may be a good task to delegate. Deciding how your business should position itself in the market is something you may want to keep in-house.

Let's look at these opportunities in more detail.

1. Content Creation and Management

Content can take more time than many businesses expect.

A single blog post may involve topic research, keyword research, writing, editing, formatting, images, publishing, and promotion.

You can outsource some or all of these tasks.

Tasks You Can Delegate

  • Blog topic research
  • Keyword research
  • Blog writing
  • Editing and proofreading
  • Content formatting
  • Blog publishing
  • Content updates
  • Content repurposing
  • Content calendar management

For example, you may decide which topics your business wants to cover while an outside marketing professional handles the research, writing, formatting, and publishing.

This allows you to stay involved in the overall direction without doing every step yourself.

2. SEO Tasks

SEO includes both strategy and day-to-day execution.

Some SEO work can be outsourced, especially when the process is clear.

Tasks You Can Delegate

  • Keyword research
  • SEO content writing
  • Title and meta description updates
  • Content optimization
  • Internal linking
  • Content research
  • Basic on-page SEO
  • SEO reporting

You may still want to keep control over your larger SEO goals and business priorities.

For example, you might decide which products or services are most important to promote. A marketing professional can then help create and optimize content around those priorities.

3. Social Media Management

Social media can become a recurring workload because businesses need to plan, create, schedule, publish, and monitor content.

You can outsource many of the routine tasks.

Tasks You Can Delegate

  • Content research
  • Caption writing
  • Content preparation
  • Social media scheduling
  • Publishing
  • Basic engagement monitoring
  • Performance reporting
  • Content calendar management

Virtual assistants and other marketing professionals commonly provide social media support as part of their services.

You should still provide clear brand guidelines and approve important content when needed.

Sensitive customer conversations, major announcements, and posts related to important business decisions may require your direct involvement.

4. Email Marketing

Email marketing involves more than writing an email.

Someone also needs to prepare the content, format the message, schedule it, manage lists, and review results.

Tasks You Can Outsource

  • Newsletter preparation
  • Email copywriting
  • Email formatting
  • Scheduling
  • List organization
  • Basic campaign reporting
  • Email content research

You may want to keep control over your main offers, promotions, and important customer communications.

An outside marketing professional can handle much of the execution while you approve the final message.

5. Website Updates

You don't always need a developer for simple website content work.

Depending on your website, you may be able to outsource tasks such as:

  • Updating website copy
  • Publishing blog posts
  • Adding images
  • Updating service information
  • Formatting pages
  • Uploading content
  • Checking links
  • Making basic content changes

Larger technical changes, custom development, or security-related work may require a qualified developer.

The important point is to separate content management from technical development.

6. Lead Generation Support

Lead generation can also involve many repetitive tasks.

For example, a business may need someone to research potential customers, organize information, and keep its CRM up to date.

Potential Tasks to Outsource

  • Prospect research
  • Lead list building
  • Contact research
  • Data entry
  • CRM updates
  • Lead organization
  • Basic lead qualification support

The sales team or business owner can still decide which prospects are a good fit and how those prospects should be approached.

This can reduce the amount of research and administrative work your team needs to handle.

7. Marketing Research

Research is another area where outside support can save time.

You may need to understand your audience, competitors, industry, or potential content topics.

Research Tasks You Can Delegate

  • Competitor research
  • Audience research
  • Keyword research
  • Topic research
  • Industry research
  • Content research
  • Market research support

The person doing the research can collect and organize the information.

Your internal team can then use that information to make business and marketing decisions.

8. Marketing Reporting and Analytics

Marketing reports can take time to prepare, especially when information comes from several platforms.

You can outsource parts of the reporting process, such as:

  • Collecting marketing data
  • Updating reports
  • Organizing campaign results
  • Preparing dashboards
  • Tracking content performance
  • Creating recurring reports

However, collecting the data and deciding what the data means are two different things.

An outside professional can help prepare the information. Your team may still need to decide what actions to take based on the results.

What Marketing Tasks Should a Small Business Keep In-House?

Outsourcing does not mean handing over your entire marketing operation.

Some responsibilities are closely connected to your business and may be better kept under your control.

These can include:

  • Business strategy
  • Brand positioning
  • Pricing decisions
  • Core messaging
  • Major marketing decisions
  • Customer relationships
  • Sensitive business information
  • Final approval of important content
  • Major campaign decisions

For example, you may hire someone to write your website content, but you should still have a say in what your business stands for and how you want customers to see it.

Think of outsourcing as delegating execution, not giving up ownership.

How to Decide Which Marketing Tasks to Outsource First

If you have a long list of marketing tasks, don't try to outsource everything at once.

Start by looking for tasks that are repetitive, clearly defined, time-consuming, and easy to review. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce also recommends looking at which tasks take time away from your core business and could be handled by outside support.

Start by looking for tasks that meet several of these conditions.

1. The Task Is Repetitive

Does someone need to do it every week or every month?

Recurring work can be a strong candidate for delegation.

2. The Process Is Clear

Can you explain exactly what needs to be done?

If you can create a simple process or checklist, it may be easier for someone else to take over.

3. The Task Takes Significant Time

Look at where you or your team spend the most time.

A task that takes several hours every week may be worth delegating if it does not require your direct involvement.

4. The Task Is Easy to Review

Can you quickly check whether the work was completed correctly?

If the answer is yes, the task may be easier to outsource.

5. The Task Does Not Require Major Business Decisions

Tasks that require your business knowledge, judgment, or customer relationships may need to stay with you.

Tasks that mainly involve straightforward execution are often easier to delegate.

Should You Hire a Marketing VA, Freelancer, or Agency?

There isn't one option that works for every business. If you're considering a virtual assistant, the U.S. Chamber of Commerce recommends evaluating your business needs and identifying the tasks you can delegate before deciding who to hire.

The choice depends on the type of work, how often you need it, and how much specialized knowledge the work requires.

Option May Be a Good Fit For
Marketing virtual assistant Recurring marketing support and execution
Freelancer Specific projects or specialized tasks
Specialist Work that requires deeper expertise
Marketing agency Broader marketing strategy and execution

A marketing virtual assistant may be useful when you need ongoing help with tasks such as research, content support, website updates, social media scheduling, or marketing administration.

A freelancer or specialist may make more sense when you need a specific skill for a defined project.

An agency may be appropriate when you need broader strategy and execution across several marketing areas.

The important thing is to define the work before deciding who should handle it.

How to Start Outsourcing Your Marketing

You don't need to outsource your entire marketing operation on day one.

A simple approach is to start with one clearly defined task.

Step 1: List Your Recurring Marketing Tasks

Write down everything you or your team currently handle.

Include small tasks that may only take a few minutes at a time.

Step 2: Estimate How Much Time They Take

Look for tasks that regularly consume your time.

You may find that several small tasks add up to a significant workload.

Step 3: Separate Strategy From Execution

Ask yourself:

“Does this task require me to make an important business decision, or does it mainly require someone to carry out a process?”

Tasks that mainly involve execution may be better outsourcing candidates.

Step 4: Choose One Task to Delegate

Start with something specific.

For example:

“Research and prepare two blog posts each month.”

is easier to delegate than:

“Handle all of our marketing.”

Step 5: Document the Process

Create simple instructions that explain:

  • What needs to be done
  • When it needs to be done
  • What tools should be used
  • What the finished work should look like
  • Who approves the work

Clear instructions make recurring work easier to hand off. Guidance on delegation also commonly recommends defining clear outcomes and documenting repeatable processes.

Step 6: Review and Improve

After the first few tasks, look at what worked and what didn't.

You may need to improve the instructions, adjust the workflow, or decide that another task is better suited for outsourcing.

Common Mistakes When Outsourcing Marketing

Outsourcing can make your workload easier, but only if the work is set up properly.

Outsourcing Without Clear Instructions

If someone doesn't know what success looks like, the result may not match your expectations.

Outsourcing Too Much at Once

Giving someone ten unrelated tasks on day one can make the process difficult for everyone.

Start with clearly defined responsibilities.

Giving Up All Oversight

Delegation doesn't mean disappearing.

You should still review important work and make the decisions that require your business knowledge.

Choosing Support Based Only on the Lowest Price

The cheapest option may not always be the right fit.

Look at the person's skills, communication, experience, and ability to follow your process.

Expecting One Person to Do Everything

Marketing includes many different skills.

Someone who is good at content writing may not be the right person to manage complex paid advertising campaigns.

Match the person to the work. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce also notes that areas such as writing, social media marketing, and web design involve different skills.

Final Takeaway

Small businesses don't need to handle every marketing task internally.

Content, SEO support, social media, email marketing, website updates, lead generation, research, and reporting can all contain tasks that are suitable for outsourcing.

The goal isn't to hand over your entire marketing operation.

The goal is to identify the work that doesn't need to stay on your plate.

Start with a recurring task that is easy to define and review. Keep your core strategy and important business decisions under your control, then delegate the execution that someone else can handle effectively.
