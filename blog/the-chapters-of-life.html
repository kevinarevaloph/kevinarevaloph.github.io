PERSONAL GROWTH & FAITH

The Chapters of Life

A personal reflection on childhood, growth, challenges, and faith

A person's life is like a chapter in a book series. It depends on us how we make each chapter beautiful. God writes the book with us. He holds the pen, while we hold the paper.

In this world, there are many kinds of paper: smooth, rough, thick, fragrant, and many more. If we offer God good "paper" in our lives, He can write a beautiful story in every chapter of our lives.

The first chapter of our life begins the moment we are born into this world. We are fresh, clean, and innocent. We don't know what to do, and we cannot speak yet. We cry when we feel something, stay quiet when nothing hurts, and smile when the people around us make us happy.

As we grow older, we begin learning the things we need to know about the world. We start by saying simple words, usually "Mama" and "Papa," because they are the first people we trust to take care of us, give us a good life, and love us.

But as we continue learning about the world around us, we also begin to see things that aren't always good. From everything we see and experience, whether good or bad, we learn, grow, and change.

A child's heart and mind start out pure and spotless. As we grow, they slowly become filled with the experiences we go through. This is also when we begin choosing the people we want to be around and building relationships with others.

I wrote this blog because I want to share God's plan for my life. As I grow older, I am learning to understand the problems, challenges, and experiences I have faced and those I may face in the future.

I have realized one simple truth: God loves all of us deeply. He will never leave us or let us face anything alone.

As people who help shape our own lives, we need guidance from the author of our life story. We need His direction so we can learn to value our lives and fill them with meaning and color. With His guidance, we can turn our lives into a beautiful story before each chapter comes to an end.

Chapter I: When I Was a Child

When I was young, I loved drawing and designing things around me. My siblings and I used to build a small playhouse, and I really enjoyed decorating it. We built our little "playhouse" out of plywood and placed it on top of the wooden bed frame we slept on.

We didn't grow up in a wealthy family. We slept on a papag that our father built. He placed pieces of plywood on top, cut to match our height and width. The plywood had just enough extra space so we wouldn't roll over the edges while we slept.

My childhood was happy even though we weren't wealthy. Happiness depends on how a person chooses to see their life and how they choose to make it better.

Finding Happiness in Simple Things

We create our own joy, so let's not waste each day thinking only about the negative things. Worrying too much about what might happen tomorrow can lead to anxiety and sadness.

As the Bible says in Matthew 6:34 (NIV):

"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."

Even if you have a problem that feels impossible to solve in just one day, try to do at least one small thing that can help lessen it today.

If you continue reducing your problems little by little each day and keep taking small steps, you may eventually look back and realize, "Oh, I've already solved it."

And as you work through your problems each day, remember that you need support too. Through prayer, ask God for wisdom, guidance, and strength so you can overcome even a part of your burden today.

Growing Through Family Challenges

I was a child who faced family problems at an early age. Not necessarily problems with our parents, but challenges caused by some of the relatives around us.

My siblings and I grew up in a noisy and chaotic environment. We argued a lot and fought often. There were times when things became so difficult that some of our relatives almost wanted to give up on us. The truth is, there were even times when they felt ashamed of us as their nieces and nephews.

In a huge family, arguments are normal. Some people get into fights, while others sometimes cause them. My mom was often the one who ended up being blamed, and many of our relatives did not really like her.

We have lived with my dad's side of the family from the beginning until now. All of us stay on the same lot, which is more than 100 square meters. Each family has its own house, but all the houses are built on the same property.

Four families live there in total: my grandfather's family of four, my aunt's family of four, my uncle's family of four, and our family of six. There are four of us siblings, and I am the youngest.

I have witnessed the unpleasant things that happened in our home from the very beginning. My father's siblings would often team up and argue with our mother.

My mom is an introvert. She usually talks only when someone starts a conversation with her. Because of this, some of our relatives often thought she was unfriendly or difficult to get along with.

My mom also speaks very directly. Sometimes she says things without a filter, and she points out mistakes when she sees them. I never fully understood why our relatives sometimes felt like they were being attacked when they received even small comments or corrections from my parents.

These misunderstandings would often lead to arguments and end with people exchanging hurtful words.

Perhaps this was also one of the reasons why some of our relatives did not like us very much. Maybe they thought that the way we behaved was a reflection of how our mother raised us.

I also never understood what they used as a basis for judging a person's intelligence. I admit that my siblings and I were not the smartest children, but we were not stupid or foolish either. We were simply very playful, mischievous, and energetic when we were young.

Maybe because of this, we sometimes heard hurtful words from them. They would call us stupid, say that we had no discipline, and even compare us to our cousins who were kind, intelligent, and well-behaved.

My siblings and I simply learned to let those words pass. We didn't spend too much time thinking about the hurtful things we heard from them.

As I mentioned earlier, our childhood was happy.

Chapter II: What I Didn't Like Became Part of My Growth

Why did I choose this title?

I chose this title because this is where I truly realized that in a person's life, no matter what we go through, everything that happens has a purpose. Whether the experience is good or bad, every experience teaches us something.

Everything in this world, whether it is alive or not, has a purpose. Everything has a reason for why God created it or why it exists.

So, why did I choose this title for this chapter?

Learning to Go to School

I was the kind of child who really hated going to school. Even when I was in kindergarten, I would cry whenever my mother left me at school. But because I was very scared of my mother back then, I would quietly cry while thinking about what I would do if I could no longer see her.

Whenever my mother saw me crying while I was standing in line with my section and waiting for our teacher, she would give me a very serious look and say, "Try crying again, and just wait until we get home."

Even when I could not clearly hear her voice, I knew exactly what she was saying. I could tell by the way she moved her mouth with every word, and I knew she was angry with me.

Today, it is funny to think about and tell this story. But at that time, the fear I felt was very real, and it was a very different experience for me.

Eventually, I got used to going to school every day. There even came a time when I could walk to school by myself and go home by myself after class. Our elementary school was close to our house, so it was within walking distance.

Elementary school was also when I learned how to read and write. It is actually funny because I could read very quickly. But whenever someone asked me what was happening in the story I had just read, I often had no idea.

I didn't really understand what I was reading. What mattered to me back then was that I could read fast.

Growing Up in the Philippines

Here in the Philippines, we often experience typhoons. Having around ten typhoons enter the country in one year is already considered a small number.

So whenever there was a typhoon, I was very happy because classes were suspended. It felt so good to wake up on a quiet morning, eat breakfast, and watch cartoons.

In the afternoon, if the rain was still strong, my siblings and friends would invite each other outside to play and swim in the rain. Of course, asking our parents for permission was always a challenge.

But whenever we asked our father, he would usually let us go right away. He would simply tell us that after playing in the rain, we had to take a proper bath inside the house so we would not get sick.

I still don't know why, but when we got soaked by the rain in the Philippines, it seemed like we would easily get a cold, cough, or fever if we didn't take a bath right away afterward.

High School and the Lessons I Didn't Expect

Eventually, I graduated from elementary school even though I really didn't like going to school.

To me, school felt like a mandatory part of being a child. It was something you had to do whether you liked it or not. You had no other choice but to go to school.

By God's grace, I still passed my classes even though I was quite lazy when it came to studying.

People often say that "high school is the happiest journey of a student's life." But for me, it still wasn't.

I still didn't like going to school.

The routine was always the same: go to school, go home, and repeat. It felt like the same thing over and over again.

I made many friends at school, though. Eventually, I even learned how to drink alcohol when I was in my first year of high school.

My friends back then were definitely a bad influence on me. But I am still happy that they became part of my life. I had fun being with them because, in a strange way, they helped me become more confident and brave.

There were also many times when I didn't actually go to school. Instead, I would go straight to my classmate's house, which was almost directly across from our school, and we would drink there.

I also tried smoking, but my lungs and especially my taste buds did not like it at all. The taste was terrible, and I found it disgusting.

During that time, I also experienced getting into fights outside the school and joining in street fights. But somehow, none of the stupid things I did back then ever reached my parents.

Even though I was a troublemaker at that time, I still tried to be careful. I made sure that I would not cause problems for my parents.

A Teacher Who Changed My Perspective

Eventually, I finished my first year of high school. Since I often skipped school, my grades were not very good. But at least, I still passed.

When I started my second year of high school, I was placed in a lower section. There were also many students there who were a bad influence.

But I don't know why or how it happened, but something suddenly changed in me.

We had a male teacher who was very strict and intimidating. He was angry with students who were lazy or rebellious, and he was also openly homophobic.

All of his students were afraid of him. His voice was extremely loud whenever he spoke. You would think he was a six-foot-tall man because of how powerful his voice sounded.

But in reality, he was only about 4'11".

There were many times when he would force male students who wore contact lenses or makeup to leave the classroom. He would take their bags and throw them outside. He would also force them to remove their contact lenses, put the lenses inside a bottle of water, and shake the bottle until the lenses were torn apart.

Of course, sometimes I felt sorry for my classmates. But I didn't know what I could do. I was afraid that if I defended them, I might become his next target.

So I focused on myself.

I did all the assignments and reports he gave us.

I really didn't like him. I didn't like the way he treated other students because I knew it was wrong.

But something unexpected happened.

Because I kept following his instructions and completing everything he asked us to do, he started to notice me.

The History Assignment

Almost the entire class, and even some students from his other sections, had already written the whole book for his subject.

His subject was History.

I should also mention that our school was a public school with many students. Because of this, two sections were sometimes combined for one subject.

During the time he became my teacher, our lower section was combined with the school's top section, which was called SPA (Special Program in the Arts). They were even considered higher than Section 1.

So why did this teacher notice and like me as one of his students?

Aside from the fact that I came from one of the lower sections, he did not expect much from me. It was almost as if he thought I did not belong where I was.

But my exam scores in his subject were high.

The Prime Minister of China Assignment

There was also one experience that I will never forget.

One day, we were given an assignment to complete at home. Our teacher's instruction was simple: draw the face of the Prime Minister of China.

As a lazy student, I went home and immediately fell asleep because I was tired. I woke up in the afternoon when it was almost evening, and I had completely forgotten about my History assignment.

I only remembered it the next morning.

My friends and I were sitting in the school canteen when I saw the SPA students who were also taking History with us. They all had printed pictures of the Prime Minister of China.

That was when I suddenly remembered:

Oh, that was our History assignment!

At that time, I didn't have money to print anything. Color printing was also expensive back then, and I had already spent my allowance on food.

So I decided to draw it myself.

I asked for a blank piece of white paper. I borrowed a pencil and an eraser. Then I found someone who had a picture of the Prime Minister of China that I could copy.

Within one hour, I finished the drawing.

It actually turned out really well.

My drawing was detailed and looked good enough that even my friends were amazed at how well I could draw a person's face.

I simply told them that drawing was one of my hobbies. Whenever I had nothing to do at home, I would draw, or sleep the whole afternoon.

The Moment My Teacher Noticed Me

Then it was finally time for our History class.

Before entering the classroom, our teacher collected our assignments. I was extremely happy when he accepted my work. At least I had something to submit.

Eventually, all the students had entered the classroom. Some students were not allowed to come inside because they had nothing to submit.

Everyone became quiet.

Our teacher slowly walked toward the middle of the classroom. Then he separated my drawing from all the other papers.

That was when I became extremely nervous.

I was the only student who had submitted a drawing.

My feet and hands became cold. I started sweating even though the classroom was very hot.

Then our teacher suddenly spoke.

He asked, "What was my instruction?"

He called one student to the front and asked him to read the assignment.

The student answered, "Draw a picture of the Prime Minister of China."

Then our teacher asked the entire class, "So, what did all of you submit to me?"

No one said anything.

Suddenly, our teacher started tearing up all the papers in front of everyone.

Except mine.

He picked up my paper and called my name.

"Kevin!"

He told me that I was the only student who followed his instructions.

That was when I finally relaxed.

At that moment, I felt like I was a special student to him.

Then he looked at the SPA students and said that they were supposed to be the top section, but they still could not follow a simple instruction.

He pointed to me, a student from one of the lowest sections, and said that I was the only one who followed his instructions.

I was extremely happy.

That was also the moment when he truly noticed me and got to know me as a student.

Discovering That I Could Do More Than I Thought

It was strange to think about.

I used to hate going to school. I didn't even have much interest in studying. Most of the time, I simply followed my teachers' instructions even when I didn't really want to.

But somehow, I still managed to pass.

By the time I reached my third year of high school, I had moved up to one of the top sections. When I reached fourth year, I was still in one of the top sections.

Eventually, I graduated from high school as a student from one of the top sections.

But honestly, my main goal was never to become an honor student.

I just wanted to pass.

What These Chapters Taught Me

Looking back at these parts of my life, I can see how every experience became part of my story.

My childhood taught me that happiness does not always come from having money or living a perfect life. Sometimes happiness comes from the simplest things: playing with your siblings, drawing, building a small playhouse, spending time with friends, or simply enjoying a rainy afternoon.

My family experiences taught me that people will sometimes judge you based on what they see or what they believe about you. Not every judgment will be fair, and not every hurtful word deserves to define who you are.

School taught me something else. Even when we don't understand why we are going through something, the experience may still become part of our growth.

I didn't like going to school. I didn't think I was a great student. I made mistakes. I made bad decisions. I skipped classes. I was influenced by the wrong people.

But along the way, I discovered things about myself that I didn't know before.

I discovered that I could draw. I discovered that I could follow instructions. I discovered that I could perform well in subjects I didn't think I was good at. Most importantly, I learned that the chapter I was living at that time did not have to determine how the rest of my story would look.

Key Takeaways

  • A difficult childhood does not mean you cannot have a meaningful and happy life.
  • The experiences we go through can teach us lessons that we may not understand at first.
  • Hurtful words from other people do not have to define who we become.
  • Small talents and interests can eventually become important parts of our lives.
  • Even mistakes and difficult experiences can become part of our growth.
  • Every chapter of life can teach us something if we are willing to look back and learn from it.

Conclusion

Life is a collection of chapters. Some chapters are happy. Some are painful. Some are confusing, and some are chapters we may wish we could rewrite.

But perhaps the beauty of life is that the story is not over yet.

We may not always understand why certain things happen. We may not understand why we experience difficult relationships, failures, mistakes, disappointments, or unexpected changes. But as we continue moving forward, we may eventually see how those experiences became part of the person we are becoming.

I believe that God is the author of my life story. He holds the pen while I hold the paper.

I may not know what the next chapter will look like. I may not know what challenges are waiting for me or what wonderful experiences are still ahead.

But I want to continue giving God good "paper" to write on.

I want every chapter to have meaning, color, lessons, and purpose.

And when one chapter ends, I hope I can look back and say that even though the story wasn't perfect, it was worth living.

Because the story is still being written.

Thank You for Reading My Story

This is only one chapter of my life. I hope to continue sharing more stories, experiences, lessons, and reflections as the next chapters are written.
